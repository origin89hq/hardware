# Board B: the interlock proven, 2026-09-17

The generator contact closed under board A's control for the first time, and
dropped on its own 4.3 s after the kicks stopped. Getting there took a rework
on the relays, a remade cable, and most of a day ruling out things that were
not the fault.

## What was connected

- Board A revision A on a bench supply at 14.4 V, limit 1 A, running
  `o89-selftest` build `d71787e6` with `esp-stress`, `gen-stress`, `genboard`,
  `modbus-io` and `pzem`, debug lines included.
- Board B revision A on CN9, five wires pin 1 to pin 1, in a cable recrimped
  during the session.
- F1 fitted with a cartridge. Without one the contact chain is open at the
  fuse and a meter across CN10 reads open however well the relays close.
- Late in the session, a 12 V battery, a 1 kΩ resistor and an LED in series
  across CN10, so the contact itself is visible rather than inferred.
- The `gen-stress` feature, added today, runs the interlock sequence 10 s after
  boot and every 30 s after, instead of every 15 minutes. A rework can be
  judged in a minute rather than an afternoon.

## What I was trying to find out

Whether the relay rework closes the chains, and if not, what does. The
2026-09-14 session left both chains wired from each pole's NC terminal to its
NO terminal with the commons unconnected, so neither the generator contact nor
`FEEDBACK` could close.

## What happened

Four links per board, 200 mil each, on the solder side: `4` to `6` and `13` to
`11` on K1 and again on K2. That ties each pole's common to the trace that
enters at its NC terminal, so energising the coil closes common to NO and the
chain conducts. K2 sits rotated 90 degrees from K1 on the layout, so the straps
run vertically on one and horizontally on the other.

That alone did not make the test pass. For most of the day the sequence failed
with code 1, did not close, on the reworked board and on an untouched one
alike, with relay B's LED lit and relay A's dark. Relay A follows `WD_OK` from
the monostable, so the timer was never releasing.

The cause was the cable. Its crimp terminals had been made with ferrules
inside them. A JST VH terminal works by cold-welding bare strands; a ferrule is
a hard tube the wings cannot bite through, so the joint is a sleeve loosely
pinched in a barrel. Three conductors happened to conduct, which is why board B
powered up and `RUN` arrived and relay B closed. `KICK` did not, board B's R2
held that line low, the monostable never saw a rising edge, and the symptom
followed the cable rather than the board. Recrimping without ferrules fixed it.

Since then, **68 sequences have passed and none has failed**. Every one of the
166 recorded failures predates the rework and the recrimp.

Dropout, measured from the last kick's rising edge to the contact opening:

| When | Dropout |
|---|---|
| First pass, 13:05 | 4464 ms |
| Five-minute soak, 13:13 to 13:17 | 4394, 4383, 4383, 4378, 4378, 4378, 4379, 4374 ms |
| With the contact LED fitted, 13:57 to 13:59 | 4345, 4344, 4340, 4344 ms |

The specification is 3.0 to 6.5 s, and the design figure is
0.45 × R3 × C3 = 0.45 × 1 MΩ × 10 µF ≈ 4.5 s. Every measurement sits inside a
120 ms band that drifts gently downward through the afternoon. Warming was the
obvious explanation, and the endurance runs below complicate it.

The full sequence passes: the contact closes within 3 s of `RUN` going high
with kicks arriving, stays closed while they continue, opens 4.3 s after the
last one, closes again when kicks resume, and opens at once when `RUN` drops.

## What surprised me

How much of the day went to things that were not the fault. Board A was proven
to be driving both lines correctly by sampling GPIOD over SWD while a sequence
ran: 488 samples caught `RUN` high alone and `RUN` high with `KICK` high, which
is the pulse. The flying-probe exports cleared both boards' routing, including
a scare where board A's CN9 pins 3 and 4 sit on placeholder nets `NET_7` and
`NET_8` — those turned out to have two pads each, the MCU pin and the connector
pin, so they are unnamed in the schematic source but routed in copper. The
74HC123's own datasheet confirmed the design's pin usage, and the HT7533's
confirmed the regulator's. All correct, all irrelevant: the fault was in a
crimp.

Two lessons worth keeping. A ferrule in a crimp terminal produces exactly the
failure that is hardest to diagnose, an intermittent that follows the cable
across board swaps. And a summarised datasheet is not a datasheet: a pin table
fetched as prose claimed pin 13 of the 74HC123 was a reset input, when reading
the PDF directly showed it is the active-high output this board depends on.

## What became a test

`gen-stress` is now a firmware feature rather than a temporary edit. It implies
`genboard`, and carries the same warning: nothing on board B's CN10, because
the contact closes every 30 s.

The LED across CN10 turns the interlock into something you can watch. Relay B's
LED shows the controller asking, relay A's shows the watchdog agreeing, and the
contact LED shows the result. Stop the kicks and the last two go out together
4.3 s later, which is the safety argument made visible: the contact can close
only while the controller is both asking and actively kicking.

## The endurance runs

The sequence proves the transitions in about twenty seconds. A generator runs
for hours, so `gen-hold` was written this evening: after a passing sequence it
closes the contact again and holds it for four hours, kicking at 1 Hz and
sampling `FEEDBACK` every millisecond, failing on any open however brief. The
dropout it records is measured at the end of those hours rather than seconds
after the contact first closed.

Board A ran `o89-selftest` with `esp-stress`, `gen-stress`, `genboard`,
`modbus-io`, `pzem` and `gen-hold`, debug lines included, on a supply at
14.4 V with a 1 A limit. The counters, logbook and event ring were wiped
before the run, so the figures below are this run alone. Board B on CN9 with
its four straps and the recrimped cable, F1 fitted, and a 12 V battery, a
1 kΩ resistor and an LED across CN10.

**The contact stayed closed for four hours with no open recorded**, and again
for a second and a third four hours after it. Three sequences, three passes, no
failure anywhere on either board across twelve hours of continuously closed
contact. The dropouts were **4291 ms**, **4267 ms** and **4232 ms**:

| When | Dropout |
|---|---|
| First pass, 13:05 | 4464 ms |
| Five-minute soak, 13:13 to 13:17 | 4394 down to 4374 ms |
| With the contact LED fitted, 13:57 to 13:59 | 4345 down to 4340 ms |
| **After a four-hour hold, 20:50** | **4291 ms** |
| **After a second four-hour hold, 00:50** | **4267 ms** |
| **After a third four-hour hold, 04:50** | **4232 ms** |

Every one sits inside the 3.0 to 6.5 s window and below the design figure of
0.45 × 1 MΩ × 10 µF ≈ 4.5 s, and each is lower than the one before it: the
third is 232 ms below the first measurement of the day and 108 ms below the
best of the afternoon. The obvious reading after the first hold was the
monostable's RC timing following board temperature, since R3 and C3 sit beside
the relays and four hours of continuous coil dissipation warms that region far
more than a twenty-second sequence does.

The two holds that followed rule out board warming as the *sole* explanation.
Each came back shorter than the one before while the bench got steadily cooler:
ambient fell from 25.8 °C in the afternoon to 24.7 °C by morning, and K1 with
it, from 40.1 to 40.4 °C during the first hold to 39.0 to 39.6 °C during the
third. Warming that shortens the pulse cannot be what is happening when the
drift continues as the board cools.

That is not the same as ruling temperature out, and this record does not
support the stronger claim. What was measured is bench ambient and K1's case.
The timing is set by R3 and C3, whose own temperature was never measured and
whose coefficients are not established here. C3 sits beside relays dissipating
about 1.4 W continuously, so its local temperature is driven mostly by that
rather than by the room, and may barely have moved across the three holds. An
X7R's capacitance against temperature is a curve rather than a line, so even
the sign of the effect is an assumption until somebody measures it.

What replaces the explanation is not settled. Ceramic aging in C3 fits the
direction: an X7R's capacitance falls logarithmically with time since it last
passed its Curie point, and B-07 already singles C3 out as the part whose value
has to be known. But aging predicts decrements that shrink as the hours
accumulate, and these did not, 24 ms between the first hold and the second and
then 35 ms between the second and the third. With one measurement per four
hours, against consecutive afternoon readings that varied by 20 ms on their
own, scatter alone could produce that. The honest position is that board
warming alone does not account for it, and nothing yet does. The total drift is
232 ms, 5.2 %, over about sixteen hours, all of it inside the 3.0 to 6.5 s
window. A run that measures the dropout hourly, with a probe on C3 rather than
on a relay case, would separate the two.

## What the relays did thermally

A DS18B20 taped to K1's case gave board B its first temperature measurement.
It rose to **40.1 °C** in about eighteen minutes and then stopped, holding 39.6
to 40.4 °C for the remaining three and a half hours against a bench ambient of
25.5 °C. A rise of 14.6 °C at roughly 0.7 W per coil puts the case-to-air path
near 20 °C/W.

Both coils energised continuously is new: the plain sequence holds them for
about twenty seconds every fifteen minutes. K1 and K2 are `G5V-2-DC12`, 41.7 mA
and 288 Ω from Omron's table, so 0.5 W per coil at 12 V and about 0.71 W each
on this bench's 14.2 V rail. The relay is rated to 65 °C ambient and ran at
25.5 °C, and B-14 records the two sitting 0.3 mm apart where they warm each
other; the measured plateau says that combination is comfortable. The case is
not the winding, which runs hotter inside the plastic, so 40 °C is a trend and
an alarm threshold rather than a coil temperature. Overnight it tracked the
room down, holding 39.0 to 39.9 °C as the bench fell to 24.4 °C by morning, so
the rise above ambient barely moved across the twelve hours.

## The rest of the board

Twelve hours forty-seven minutes, one boot, no resets, no failure in any test:
CAN 46 012, RS-485 44 121, VE.Direct 9 206, analogue 9 204, selector 4 597,
probes 4 266, FRAM 761, crystals and RTC 746, ESP32 634 banners over 634 rail
cycles, NOR 78. Crystal drift −13 ppm. The Modbus module answered 45 958 of
45 959 polls, the single miss being the first poll of the boot, which arrives
before the module has finished starting; it has not grown in 46 000 attempts.

## Still open

- These boards need the four straps each. The rework is proven, but revision B
  should fix the relay footprint so the chains run through the commons.
- Brown-out and surge behaviour of the contact pair, and requirement B-04's
  ask to watch `WD_OK` while the contact makes and breaks, since revision A's
  ground pour runs under the contact region.
- The interlock has now held through three four-hour holds with no open and no
  failure, twelve hours of closed contact, but all of it near 25 °C. A cold
  night is still untested, and the dropout has drifted monotonically from
  4464 ms to 4232 ms while the bench cooled, which rules out board warming as
  the sole cause but not temperature itself: R3 and C3's own temperature was
  never measured, and an X7R's coefficient is a curve whose sign nobody has
  established here. An hourly dropout measurement with a probe on C3 would
  separate ceramic aging, local temperature and scatter before anyone trusts
  the 3.0 to 6.5 s window across a February.
- The second board, without straps, still cannot close either chain. It is
  useful as a control and should stay that way until revision B.
