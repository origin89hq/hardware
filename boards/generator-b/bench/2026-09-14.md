# Board B, first boards: first contact with board A, 2026-09-14

One of the five assembled boards from the 2026-09-09 export.

## What was connected

- CN9 to board A revision A's CN9 through a five-wire VH cable, plugged in
  with board A powered. Whether the cable was metered pin to pin first was not
  recorded.
- No cartridge in F1. Nothing on CN10.
- Board A ran `o89-selftest` built with `--features genboard` (the firmware
  repository, uncommitted), from its 12 V bench supply at about 11.9 V.

## What I was trying to find out

Bring-up step 7, point 1: whether `RUN` high with kicks at 1 Hz closes both
relays and pulls `FEEDBACK` low at board A, and then the watchdog drop-out
time.

## What happened

- Plugging CN9 into a running board A did not reset it: its boot counter did
  not move and its 12 V input read 11.87 V afterwards.
- Board A's sequence starts 60 s after boot: `RUN` high, a 100 ms kick every
  second, and up to 3 s for `FEEDBACK` to read low. On boot 26 and again on
  boot 27 it logged `genboard: sequence Fail(1)`: `FEEDBACK` never read
  closed.
- During both runs both LEDs lit, and the relays were heard to click when the
  coils were energized and again when they were released. Seen and heard, not
  measured.
- CN9's nets match pin for pin in the two boards' 2026-09-09 flying-probe
  exports: 1 `+12V`/`V12`, 2 `GND`, 3 `RUN`/`GEN_RUN_CMD` (PD0), 4
  `KICK`/`GEN_WDT_KICK` (PD1), 5 `FEEDBACK`/`GEN_STATUS` (PD2). Board A's
  firmware reads PD2 with its internal pull-up and drives PD0 and PD1 as
  outputs.
- In board B's export, K1 and K2 use pin 1 (`+12V`), pin 16 (coil low), pins
  6 and 8 in the contact chain (`GEN_A_F`, `GEN_MID`, `GEN_B`) and pins 11 and
  9 in the feedback chain (`GND`, `FB_MID`, `FEEDBACK`). Pins 4 and 13 of both
  relays are on no other net.
- Omron's G5V-2 terminal arrangement (bottom view; catalogue K046-E1-03,
  page 108, and K046-E1-06, page 4) is: coil 1 and 16; pole 1 COM 4, NC 6,
  NO 8; pole 2 COM 13, NC 11, NO 9.

## What surprised me

Both chains run from a pole's normally-closed terminal to its normally-open
one and never touch the common. NC and NO are never connected to each other,
only each to COM, so neither the contact toward CN10 nor the feedback to
board A can close, whatever the coils do. That is the fault board A
reported. [GENERATOR-BOARD.md](../GENERATOR-BOARD.md) named pins 6 and 11 as
the commons. The failure is in the safe direction: the generator contact stays
open. It also means this revision cannot start a generator at all.

The drive side works as far as eye and ear can tell: `RUN`, `KICK`, the
watchdog, both drivers and both coils.

## What became a test

Nothing new. Board A's `genboard` sequence already covers step 7, points 1 to
4, and reported this as fail 1.

## Still open

- Confirm the pinout on a fitted relay with a meter, board unpowered: pin 4
  to pin 6 closed at rest, pin 6 to pin 8 never closed.
- Rework on these boards, not yet tried: on each relay's solder side, link
  pin 4 to pin 6 and pin 13 to pin 11. With NC tied to COM, the terminal the
  board uses follows COM, so each chain closes only while its coil is
  energized. After it, CN10 can close: keep CN10 empty for bench tests.
- Then step 7, points 1 to 7, and the measured drop-out time.
- Next revision: put the contact and feedback nets on pins 4 and 13, check the
  G5V-2 symbol's pin names in the EasyEDA library, and correct
  GENERATOR-BOARD.md.
- F1's cartridge is not in the BOM; B-05 asks for a 5 × 20 mm, 2 A fuse.
- The relay wiring is tracked in
  [origin89hq/hardware#7](https://github.com/origin89hq/hardware/issues/7).
