# Board A, revision A: first overnight soak, 2026-09-15 to 2026-09-16

The same revision A board as the 2026-09-14 session, left running unattended
for 12 h 50 min with the laptop and the debug probe disconnected. This is the
first run that rests entirely on the board's own record: the FRAM counters and
logbook and the NOR event ring, read back the next morning.

## What was connected

- A 12 V battery, not a bench supply, with a PZEM-003/017 DC meter in line,
  polled by the board over RS-485 channel 3 (CN4) at address 1.
- Three DS18B20 probes on the 1-Wire bus, all three answering.
- RS-485 channels 1 and 2 (CN2, CN3) joined as a loopback pair. Channel 3
  belongs to the meter in this build, so its matrix row and column stay zero.
- CAN in external loopback, which needs no external wiring.
- VE.Direct ports 1 and 2 with nothing attached.
- The ESP32-C6 module on the board, with its rail switched by the firmware.
- No board B on CN9, and the selector reads off.
- The operator reports the board was closed in a box for the run. The ST-Link
  was unplugged, which the morning's first read confirms.

Image: `o89-selftest`, build `c5ade986`, features `esp-stress` and `pzem`,
flashed at 17:24 on 2026-09-15. Immediately after flashing, the board was reset
to a new board over SWD: counters, logbook and event ring wiped. It restarted
as boot 1, starved its watchdog on purpose to prove it, and came back as boot 2
at 17:25, which is where this record begins.

## What I was trying to find out

Whether the board runs unattended for a night without the laptop, and whether
its own record is enough to say what happened afterwards. Also whether the
ESP32 rail, which crashed the processor on switch-on during the 2026-09-14
session ([origin89hq/hardware#5](https://github.com/origin89hq/hardware/issues/5)),
survives hundreds of consecutive power cycles at the `esp-stress` cadence.

## What happened

The board ran from 17:25 on 2026-09-15 to 06:16 on 2026-09-16, 12 h 50 min,
which the logbook records as its longest run. No test failed and none errored:
the logbook's findings list is empty and every counter shows fails and errors
at zero.

Lifetime passes at the end of the run:

| Test | Passes | Expected cadence |
|---|---|---|
| CAN loopback | 46 260 | one per second |
| RS-485 | 22 902 | one per two-second turn |
| VE.Direct | 9 255 | one per five-second window |
| Analogue inputs | 9 254 | one per five seconds |
| Selector | 4 622 | debounced, on change |
| DS18B20 probes | 4 290 | one per ten seconds |
| Crystals and RTC | 751 | one per minute |
| FRAM | 765 | soak every minute |
| NOR flash | 80 | soak every ten minutes |
| ESP32 | 637 | one rail cycle per `esp-stress` period |

Each count is close to its nominal cadence over 12 h 50 min, but not exactly:
one per second implies about 46 200 CAN passes against 46 260 recorded, and one
per ten seconds implies about 4 620 probe rounds against 4 290, a 7 % deficit. A
750 ms conversion plus bus time inside each 1-Wire round would account for it,
though the interval is nominal and was not measured. Read the counts as evidence
that every task kept running, not as proof that none paused. Supporting counts:
0 watchdog feeds withheld, 0 dropped events, no stalls, and the logbook stored
766 times.

All-time extremes from the logbook, all within their pass bands:

|---|---|---|
| MCU temperature | 25.7 °C at 03:21 | 30.0 °C at 05:09 |
| Probe 1 | 23.9 °C | 24.6 °C |
| Probe 2 | 23.6 °C | 24.1 °C |
| Probe 3 | 23.4 °C | 23.9 °C |
| 3.3 V rail | 3.302 V at 05:51 | 3.333 V at 20:14 |
| House 12 V | 10.75 V at 05:51 | 11.41 V at 17:45 |
| Supply (meter) | 10.97 V at 06:16 | 11.49 V at 17:25 |
| Board current | 0.03 A | 0.12 A |
| Board power | 0.3 W | 1.3 W |
| Crystal drift | −16 ppm | −13 ppm |

Drift between the LSE-driven RTC and HSE-driven embassy time stayed between
−16 and −13 ppm, against a 200 ppm limit.

The reset history at the end: one watchdog reset, which is the proof at 17:25,
and two software resets. The first software reset is the deliberate full wipe
at 17:24. The second is at 06:16 this morning, and it is not the firmware: the
image's only software reset is the full wipe, and every record survived intact.
No power reset was recorded, so the supply never dropped for the whole night.

After the soak the bench moved from the battery to an adjustable supply, which
cost two power resets, boots 4 and 5. It also gave the first check of the house
input against a known source: the supply's display reads 13.09 V, the in-line
meter 13.06 V, and the board's own house input 12.94 V. The two references
agree to 0.03 V, and the board reads 0.15 V, or 1.1 %, below them. That is
within what a 100k/10k divider of 1 % resistors and a VDDA-referenced ADC can
do, and it says the night's battery readings were real rather than an offset.

Raising the supply to 14.39 V repeated the check: meter 14.40 V, board 14.23 V
to 14.26 V, low by the same 1 %. The offset is a constant proportion, not a
fixed voltage or a drift with input. The supply's own ammeter reads 0.038 A at
13.09 V against the meter's 0.03 A to 0.05 A and 0.4 W to 0.7 W, so all three
instruments agree on the draw and nothing else is loading the bench.

## What surprised me

The 12 V supply sagged over the night, from 11.41 V at 17:45 to 10.75 V at
05:51, and the board's own analogue input and the in-line meter agree on it
(11.49 V down to 10.97 V). The board's draw stayed between 0.03 A and 0.12 A
throughout, so the load did not change. The run was on a battery, so this is
the battery discharging rather than a fault, and the two independent readings
falling together (0.08 V apart at the start, 0.22 V at the end, the meter
sitting ahead of the board's input path) say the board measures its own supply
correctly as it drops. It stayed inside the 10 V to 16 V pass band all night.

It also means an unattended run has a deadline set by the battery, and that a
long enough one walks into the analogue check's lower limit and then the
board's brown-out.

Reconnecting the ST-Link restarted the board. Boot 3 at 06:16 is recorded as a
software reset, which is how the STM32 reports a debugger's reset request, and
it cost the uptime and nothing else: counters, logbook and event ring all
carried over. Three further attaches that morning caused no restart, so
attaching by itself does not reset the board; only the reconnection did.

637 ESP32 rail cycles passed with no failure, each needing the ROM banner
within 3 s of the rail coming up and silence with the rail down. That is a lot
of consecutive switch-ons for a board whose rail switch crashed the processor
in the 2026-09-14 session. It does not clear hardware#5: those failures
followed long off periods, and `esp-stress` only produces short ones.

## What became a test

Nothing new in the firmware. On the tooling side, o89-dev now reads the ESP32
power gate (PC5) straight out of GPIOC over SWD, alongside each snapshot read,
and reports the rail as on, off or not driven with how long it has been so.
Reading the pin rather than the image means it is right whatever the board
runs, including the flashing bridge. A recorded session confirmed it against
the firmware's own log: rail off, on 7.0 s later, off again exactly 40 s after
that, matching the check's 40 s listen window.

Later that morning o89-dev gained a switch for the firmware's debug lines, `v`
in the Flash tab or `--verbose` on the command line, which builds with
`DEFMT_LOG=debug` so defmt keeps them in the image at all. The reader also
counts the debug frames an image holds and says so when there are none, since
the key that shows them looked broken against an image built at info. Board A
was flashed with such a build, `1095cc4c` with `esp-stress` and `pzem`, at
10:17 on boot 6.

It immediately named something the log had only counted: the single UART error
every ESP32 cycle reports in its `Heard` summary is a framing error, logged as
`esp: uart Framing`, once per rail transition and three times in 80 s. The
other debug calls are error paths that stay quiet, so the log is no busier than
before.

In the afternoon the bench gained its first real RS-485 slave. A Waveshare
Modbus RTU IO 8CH, eight isolated inputs and eight open-drain outputs, went on
CN3 at its factory address 1 and 9600 8N1, with pin 3 giving the isolated side
its ground reference; the flying-probe netlist settled the pinout (`CN3_1` =
`RS485_2_A`, `CN3_2` = `RS485_2_B`, `CN3_3` = GND, and `JP2` is CN3's
terminator). A `modbus-io` build reads its inputs and outputs every second and
toggles output 8 every thirty polls, reading the coils back to confirm. With
`pzem` built too, only channel 1 is left of the loopback, so the pair test
stands down: over an empty set of listeners its all-heard check returns true,
which would have recorded passes for a test nobody ran.

It works. The coils read back `Bits(128)` and `Bits(0)` in step with each
toggle, and a 12 V pilot lamp on DO8 lights with them once `DO COM` is fed from
the supply positive, which is what these sinking outputs need and the easiest
thing to leave out.

Wiring the lamp cost the board seven power resets between 12:39 and 12:48,
visible as the lifetime power count going from 2 to 9 while the RS-485 fail
count moved only 421 to 447. The bench supply's 0.5 A limit is the likely
cause. Two things follow: the limit wants raising before anything larger than a
pilot lamp is switched, and the logbook recorded only one of those seven
resets, which is the short-boot gap below seen from the other side.

The meter then read 0.02 A where board A alone had drawn 0.03 to 0.08 A all
morning, and an ESP32 rail cycle no longer moved it. The shunt was sitting in
board A's own branch, and the bench now has three loads on one supply: board A,
the module and the lamp. The ground they share gave board A's return a way
around a branch shunt. Moved into the common negative at the supply, the meter
reads 70 to 130 mA, rising and falling with the lamp and the rail, which is the
whole bench rather than one branch of it. The morning's 1 % divider check is
unaffected: that was a voltage comparison, and the two instruments still agree
on voltage to 0.13 V.

At 13:40 the brown-outs cost the counters record. Both FRAM slots failed
validation, so the record started over: the boot count back to 1, every
lifetime tally cleared, the reset histogram cleared, and the watchdog proof
marked not done. The proof then re-armed and starved the watchdog on purpose,
as it does on a fresh record — and a power reset arrived before the watchdog's
own, so it recorded `Failed` with code 1 and, by design, will not arm again.
The fault lamp has been on since, correctly.

Two things are worth keeping from that. The proof reported that it could not
prove itself rather than passing on a reset it did not cause, which is exactly
what it was written to do. And the logbook, which lives in its own FRAM region,
came through untouched and holds the whole day: extremes across boots 6 to 19
and then 2, 450 RS-485 findings with their first and last codes, and the reset
counts. The two records failing independently is the argument for keeping two.

Separately, this morning's boot showed the ESP32 application joining the bench
Wi-Fi (channel 10, −40 dBm, 1937 ms), taking a DHCP lease in 8 ms, answering
5 of 5 gateway pings and 10 of 10 to 1.1.1.1, resolving a name in 16 ms, and
completing a 1 MB HTTP download with connect at 15 ms and first byte at 62 ms.
A BLE scan found 43 devices in 15 s. That covers part of what the 2026-09-14
log left open about the radios, on boot 3 rather than during the soak.

## Still open

- The ESP32 rail after a long off period, which is the actual hardware#5 case.
  A night of short cycles says nothing about it. The `esp-diag` build is what
  exercises it.
- Whether one brown-out can invalidate both counter slots, or only a burst of
  them. Review of the firmware afterwards found one software path that can do
  it without any torn write: `counters::store` advanced the sequence before
  writing, and did not put it back when the write failed. The sequence picks
  the slot, so a failed write to one slot sent the next attempt to the other
  and overwrote the last good copy. Fixed in origin89#12; the question of what
  a single brown-out does to the FRAM itself is still open. The A/B slots with a CRC each are meant to lose at most the newest, and
  on 2026-09-16 a run of power cuts during rewiring lost both. Worth reproducing
  deliberately with a programmable supply, cutting power at a known point in the
  60 s store cycle, before trusting the record through a weak bank in February.
- How far the battery can be taken. The analogue check fails below 10 V, and
  the board's brown-out sits somewhere below that. A run to the battery's end
  would record both: the lowest house reading stays in the logbook extremes,
  and the restart shows in the reset history as a power reset. That would also
  cover the brown-out item above, at the cost of failures in the counters that
  belong to the bench rather than the board.
- Board B's interlock, which needs the `genboard` build and board B on CN9.
- The selector switch, VE.Direct with a real device, and RS-485 on a long
  cable with idle bias
  ([origin89hq/hardware#26](https://github.com/origin89hq/hardware/issues/26)).
- Brown-out behaviour and the RTC across a power loss without a cell, as
  controlled tests. The soak itself never lost power. The afternoon's seven
  supply-limit brown-outs did cut it and showed one consequence, both FRAM
  counter slots invalidated, but they were uncontrolled, nothing was measured at
  the rail, and they say nothing about the RTC.
- A gap in the logbook after a full reset. Boot 1 exists only long enough to
  starve the watchdog, so the logbook is first stored on boot 2 and its reset
  list misses boot 1's software reset, while the FRAM counters do record it.
  The fix is to store the logbook before the watchdog proof arms.
