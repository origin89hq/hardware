# Board A, revision A: first power and first contact, 2026-09-14

One revision A board from the first JLCPCB order, blank as delivered.

## What was connected

- 12 V into CN1, pin 1 positive. The supply and its current draw were not
  recorded.
- A NUCLEO-G0B1RE as the debug probe: its ST-Link V2-1 (serial
  `0671FF565357657287183750`), both CN4 jumpers removed, CN11 pins 1 to 4
  wired to H1. H1 is 3V3, SWDIO, SWCLK, GND and CN11 is VDD, SWCLK, GND,
  SWDIO, so the wires cross. H1 carries no NRST.
- probe-rs 0.32.0 on macOS.
- At first, nothing on CN2 to CN14. Later in the session: CN2, CN3 and CN4
  joined pin 1 to pin 1 and pin 2 to pin 2, with JP1 and JP3 fitted and JP2
  off; one DS18B20 on CN8; a CR2032 on CN14. No selector and no board B.
- Last, for one test, a wire from CN11 pin 2 held by hand on U8 pad 10, the
  ESP32-C6 module's IO8.
- In the afternoon: two more DS18B20s on CN8 with the first moved to CN11, and
  board B on CN9 with no fuse in its F1 and nothing on its CN10.

## What I was trying to find out

Whether the board powers up and the STM32 answers over SWD; then whether each
circuit that needs no external wiring works: the crystals and RTC, FRAM, NOR,
CAN, the analogue inputs, the watchdog, the lamps, and the ESP32-C6's switched
rail, module and UART to the STM32; then, as wiring went on, the RS-485
channels between each other, a temperature probe, and the RTC across a power
loss on its cell; last, whether the STM32 can put the ESP32 into its serial
download mode.

## What surprised me

The first ESP32 scratch image stopped with probe-rs reporting
`Run loop exited due to a locked up core`, just after its first log line. That
image configured USART1, TX on PB6 included, before PC5 went high, and started
its DMA read immediately after. A second image with a 3 s wait between PC5
going high and the first read ran without stopping.

The self-test then panicked three times in its ESP32 check, each time on the
first reads after PC5 went high, on a value its code cannot produce: a variable
that only ever holds 0 to 7 read `0x20000000`. The watchdog reset the part each
time. That was about one in four power-ons. The HAL's DMA receive path stops
its transfer on every exit and cannot write past its buffer. After the check
was changed to test the value instead of trusting it, and the STM32's voltage
detector was armed across each power-on at about 2.96 V, 18 power-ons at 30 s
intervals showed no bad value and no dip. The detector never tripped during
the session, so its latch is not proven. The cause is open.

The STM32 cannot program the ESP32 over UART0 on this revision as built.
Holding IO9 low selects a download mode, but the ROM chose its USB-only one,
which it does when IO8 is low too, and IO8 is unconnected on this board. The
module's USB pins, IO12 and IO13, are not wired either, so the board as built
has no working path to program the ESP32. A sweep of 20 resets with IO9 low
(10 power cycles with 200 ms and 2 s off-times, 10 EN resets with the rail on)
gave USB_BOOT every time, so the floating IO8 reads low reliably. Espressif's
esptool documentation says GPIO8 must be driven high to enter the serial
bootloader and that GPIO8 low with GPIO9 low is invalid. With a wire from
CN11 pin 2, which R16 pulls up to 3.3 V through 4.7 kΩ, held on U8 pad 10, an
EN reset entered the serial download mode and the ROM answered SYNC from the
STM32, which also proves the STM32-to-ESP32 direction. The 129 resets before
it in the same run gave USB_BOOT; when the wire made contact was not recorded.
The first SYNC frame got no reply within 400 ms; esptool retries SYNC the same
way, and this was not looked into. Once the ESP32 held firmware written that
way, the firmware's own restart into download mode worked with IO8 still
unconnected, so later flashes need no wire for as long as that firmware runs.

Boot 13, the power-on, was followed within 10 s by a software reset, boot 14,
at about the time the Nucleo's USB was plugged back in. Not confirmed.

On boot 27 the drift reading jumped from −16 ppm to +4663 ppm and then fell
minute by minute, to +2544 ppm half an hour in. Multiplied back out, every
reading after the jump gives the same difference: embassy time about 4.9 s
ahead of the RTC, from one step between roughly 16:16 and 16:17, with the
crystals' rate unchanged around it. Either the 32.768 kHz crystal stopped for
about 5 s or the HSE time base gained 5 s. That boot ran board B's relay test,
whose second run was due shortly before, and board B's cable was unplugged
from CN9 at about that time with board A running. In this board's export CN9
pins 3, 4 and 5 run straight to PD0, PD1 and PD2 with no series resistor or
TVS on board A, so a connector that breaks ground before 12 V can push board
B's current into the MCU's pins; a stalled low-power 32.768 kHz oscillator
(the HAL's default LSE drive, medium-high) would lose seconds this way. That is
a likely mechanism, not a reproduced one. The self-test now logs the raw
difference every minute, so a repeat shows the minute it happened; boot 28
read 0 ms after its first 61 s.

The first long run, boot 28 from 16:33 with board B unplugged, ended at 23:41
in a watchdog reset that blamed no task: boot 29's record reads
`Boot { cause: Iwdg, blamed: None, overdue_ms: 0 }` and the feed-withheld
count stayed 0, so the supervisor itself stopped, not one slow task. The last
event from boot 28 is its minute marker at 25 699 s of uptime. The ESP32 check
runs every 642 to 647 s and its previous cycle ended at 25 103 s, so the hang
fell at an ESP32 rail-on. Over those seven hours the ESP32 test failed 34
times (104 passes and 1 fail at 16:42, 110 and 35 after the reset), and the
last four cycles in the ring each heard no ROM banner at all
(`EspBanner { count: 0 }`); boots 29 and 30 heard it again. That afternoon the
check had moved to a ring-buffered DMA receiver created and dropped every
cycle; the HAL's drop stops that receiver. The RTC stayed on its −16.7 ppm
line throughout (−430 ms after 25 681 s). Not explained; the ring's earlier
events, including the first ESP32 failure and its code, have not been read.

Boot 30 hung at its second ESP32 rail-on, and boot 31 at 00:16:32 on
15 September. For boot 31 the dashboard halted the core 3 s after the snapshot
stopped changing, inside the watchdog's window. The core sat in the HardFault
handler. The stacked frame put the fault in `__aeabi_memclr8` writing
0x20024000, the first address past RAM, called from the ESP32 check's
zero-initialisation of a 264-byte buffer. That call comes a few instructions
after PC5 goes high, with no await between, so it ran well under a millisecond
after the rail switched on. The routine was not given a bad address or length:
the end address it was working towards, R2 + R3 = 0x20001160, is the right one
for 264 bytes at 0x20001058. A translation of the routine clears correctly for
every length from 0 to 4096. The saved R1 of 0xFC is what one branch taken the
wrong way produces (the correct path leaves 0x100), after which the loop misses
its exit count and clears upward until it faults. Boot 32 then logged
`impossible search state 11 ms after power-on: carry=256 n=27`. `carry` is set
from `total.min(7)`, so that fits the same kind of fault, and that boot carried
on with fail 5. Both are wrong processor decisions within milliseconds of the
rail switching on. The voltage detector latched nothing on any logged cycle.

The rail switch is Q2, an AO3401A. R19 (100 kΩ) holds its gate off, and Q3, a
2N7002 on PC5, pulls the gate straight to ground, with no gate resistor or slew
capacitor. Q2 connects C24 (22 µF), C25 and the module to V3V3, which has four
22 µF capacitors (C11, C34, C35, C21). Charge sharing alone would pull V3V3
down by up to about 0.6 V, for the few microseconds the FET's on-resistance
allows. That is an estimate, not a measurement. The voltage detector's response
time is not stated by ST, so it may miss a pulse that short. The receive path
is the other candidate. USART1 is enabled before PC5 goes high, while the
unpowered module holds the line low, and every cycle counts at least one
receive error. The two have not been separated.

At 00:38:16 boot 32 faulted again, in different code. The core sat in the
HardFault handler with the stacked PC at 0x08003A34, in the boot sequence just
after its one call recording the watchdog proof (main.rs:251). LR was inside
the counter update that every test calls. A function return had taken a stale
return address left on the stack at boot, so either the stack pointer or the
saved address was wrong at that return. Cycle 3's rail-on was due at the same
moment, to within about a second, and no module output reached the log. Boot
33 followed.

The failures follow how long the rail had been off. The first cycle after a
reset passed on boots 29, 32 and 33, each about 13 s after the previous
rail-on. Later cycles on boots 31 and 32 each followed 600 s off, and each
corrupted or faulted; boot 28's last four cycles heard no banner. On the
morning of 14 September the `esp-stress` build, with power-ons 30 s apart, ran
18 power-ons with no bad value. The STM32 leaves the module's EN and BOOT pins
untouched, so nothing holds the switched rail up while it is off. That fits a
larger inrush into an emptier C24 better than it fits the receive path, whose
sequence is the same whatever the off-time. C24's voltage before a rail-on has
not been measured. Every fault came within milliseconds of power-on, while the
module was still in its ROM, so the module's firmware does not enter into it.
From this pattern, boot 33's second cycle, its first after 600 s off, should
fail, and it did. At 00:49:08 it faulted exactly as boot 31 had, with every
stacked register identical. So the fault hits the same instruction, with the
same wrong branch, at a fixed time after PC5 goes high.

From 01:00 to 04:50 the board ran in that loop, boots 34 to 53, and the
results split cleanly on off-time. All 19 rail-ons a few seconds after a
watchdog reset passed. All 22 rail-ons after 600 s off failed: 17 faulted at
the stale return, 2 in the clear routine, and 3 tripped the search check with
the same `carry=256 n=27`, 11 ms after power-on, each time. The voltage
detector latched on none of them.

At 04:55 the board was flashed with the `esp-stress` build, which switches the
rail on every 30 s or so, so that the soak can run. Its first rail-on came
about 5 minutes after the previous one and faulted, giving boot 55. The next
four rail-ons each passed, the last three after 32 s off.

At 07:41 the board ran an `esp-diag` build of the self-test. With the bench
scan image running on the module, the rail was switched off for 5, 20, 50,
100, 200, 500, 1000 and 2000 ms, three times each, and back on. All 24
switch-ons printed the ROM banner with `rst:0x1 (POWERON)`, 5 to 6 ms after
switch-on for off-times up to 500 ms and 8 to 9 ms after 1 and 2 s, and none
faulted. EN's RC delay therefore reset a running module even after 5 ms off.
A module drawing little current when the rail is cut, idle or asleep, lets the
rail and EN fall more slowly and was not tested
([origin89hq/hardware#14](https://github.com/origin89hq/hardware/issues/14)).

The same build then left the rail off for 600 s with PB6, the STM32's TX pin
to the module, driven high, listened with the rail off, and switched the rail
on; then it did the same with PB6 released, as a control. A watchdog reset
starts the sequence again, so it ran twice before the `esp-stress` image went
back on at about 08:16. Across both passes, 48 of 48 short switch-ons reset
cleanly. With PB6 high the module sent nothing during the first 60 s off, both
times, and the switch-on after 600 s still failed both times: once the banner
search read `carry=256 n=27` 11 ms after power-on, and once the core
HardFaulted. The control switch-on HardFaulted too. Both HardFaults (boots 56
and 57) stacked PC 0x08003C24, in the main task's boot-time code, with LR
0x00003A2F, which is not a return address: the same wrong-return class as boots
32 to 53 on the earlier image. Holding PB6 high neither woke the module nor
stopped the switch-on fault. The rail's voltage was not measured.

At about 10:00 CN4 was taken off the RS-485 loopback and wired to a USB–RS-485
adapter on the laptop. The adapter received channel 3's test frames at 115200
8N1 with valid CRCs (sequence numbers 3176 and 3179), the first time a
receiver other than board A's own heard its RS-485 output. It does not echo
its own transmissions, so it could not show the bus state during a reset
(#28). From about 10:50 the adapter received nothing on CN4 again, in either
A/B order, at either framing and with its RTS and DTR lines forced either way,
while board A's channel 3 still heard its own frames; the cause was not found.
Board A was powered down or lost power four times while the bench was rewired
(boots 60 to 63, reset cause power) and each time came back with its tests
running.

At 12:44 board A read a PZEM-017 DC meter itself, with the `pzem` build of the
self-test: RS-485 channel 3 polls Modbus function 0x04, eight registers from
0x0000, at 9600 8N2 once a second, while channels 1 and 2 keep testing each
other. The meter's A and B were on CN4 pins 1 and 2, its RS-485 side powered
from the Nucleo's 5 V and GND, its voltage terminals across the 12 V supply,
and its shunt in board A's supply lead. Of the first 120 polls, 119 returned a
valid reply with the CRC low byte first; the first, right after the reset, got
only the request's own echo, and there were no garbled replies or UART errors.
The meter read 11.60 to 11.62 V while board A's house input read 11.48 to
11.58 V, 0.3 to 1.0 % lower, inside the two instruments' tolerances (1 % for
the meter, 1 % resistors in board A's divider). The current read 30 mA (0.3 W)
with the ESP32 rail off and 90 mA (1.0 W) while it was on, following the
self-test's 30 s cycle; 30 mA is near the meter's 20 mA lower limit with a
shunt, so that figure is rough.

At 13:24 the ESP32-C6 ran a network test at boot, from a new build of the scan
image flashed through the STM32 without the IO8 wire (the scan app took
`O89DL`, the ROM entered download mode, and 931 536 bytes were written and
verified in 106 s). It joined a 2.4 GHz WPA2 network on channel 10 at −36 dBm
1.9 s after asking, took a DHCP lease, and pinged the gateway 5 of 5 (1.1 to
30.3 ms, average 7.5 ms) and 1.1.1.1 10 of 10 (13.7 to 17.5 ms, average
15.9 ms; the laptop on the same network measured 15 to 25 ms). It resolved
speed.cloudflare.com in 19 ms and downloaded 500 000 bytes over plain HTTP in
3.1 s, 1.3 Mbit/s, through a 4 KB TCP receive buffer that likely limits that
figure. The first attempt at 13:16 joined but got no lease within 10 s, which is
smoltcp's default wait before resending a DHCP DISCOVER; with the resend at 2 s
the lease followed at once. Range away from the access point and transmit
strength are untested (#16). With the self-test back on the STM32 (boot 66),
the test runs at every ESP32 rail-on and its lines reach the dashboard's log
within the 40 s the rail stays on: at 13:28 it took 8.8 s, with a lease after
2.1 s, the gateway at 1.0 to 3.4 ms and 1.1.1.1 at 16.2 to 99.1 ms (average
30.5 ms), and the same 1.3 Mbit/s download. With the receive buffer raised to
32 KB and the download to 1 000 000 bytes (13:41), the transfer ran at
2.2 Mbit/s, 1 MB in 3.7 s, with 1.1.1.1 at 14.6 to 18.3 ms (average 16.1 ms).
That is still well under what the radio's Wi-Fi 6 link should carry; the stack's
buffer settings, not the board, are the likelier limit, and they were not tuned.

While the STM32 ran the `esp-bridge` image used to flash the ESP32, LED1, LED2
and LED3 stayed fully lit. That image never configures USART2 to USART4, so the
three transceivers' DI pins float, and a MAX13487E whose DI reads low drives its
bus low (#28); the RX lamps light while RO is low. They went back to short
flickers once the self-test, which sets those UARTs up with TX idling high, was
flashed back at 13:27. This was seen by eye, not measured on A and B.

Each RS-485 channel hears its own transmission because the MAX13487E's RE̅ is
tied to ground on this board. That is expected from the schematic, and it
tests each transceiver without any wiring.

The VE.Direct receive lines have a 1 kΩ series resistor and no pull-up, so an
unplugged port floats unless the MCU pulls it up.

Two facts about the part and its HAL, not the board: embassy-stm32 0.6.0 does
not implement this part's backup registers (they live in TAMP), and the RTC's
calendar-initialised flag stays clear while the year field is 00, which is
what a backup-domain reset leaves.

## What became a test

The self-test records the reset cause and the blamed task on every boot. Its
ESP32 check configures only the receive pin, then switches the rail; it checks
the value that panicked, arms the voltage detector across each power-on, and
its `esp-stress` build cycles the rail every 30 s. Its RTC check sets a year
after 2000 so the initialised flag can show survival.

## Still open

- The wrong processor decisions at the ESP32 rail switch, tracked in
  [origin89hq/hardware#5](https://github.com/origin89hq/hardware/issues/5) (the scratch image's
  lockup, the panics, boots 28 to 32). Separate the hard-switched rail from the
  USART1 receive path. For the rail, scope V3V3 at U7 triggered on PC5, and run
  a self-checking loop with interrupts masked across the switch. For the receive
  path, configure the UART only after the rail settles. Measure C24 at the end
  of a 10-minute off period, and alternate short and long off-times in one run.
  Also read the whole event ring for boot 28's first ESP32 failure and its code.
- The IO8 pull-up, by rework on this board and in the next revision: without
  it, a blank ESP32, or one whose firmware does not run, still needs a wire
  held on U8 pad 10. Tracked in
  [origin89hq/hardware#6](https://github.com/origin89hq/hardware/issues/6).
- RS-485 on a real cable. A PZEM-017 answered 119 of 120 polls on a short
  bench lead; a long twisted pair and the idle bias (#26) are still untested.
- The selector switch, and board B's interlock, which waits on board B's relay
  wiring.
- The RTC across a power loss without a cell, and for longer than 10 s.
- Brown-out behaviour.
- The 4.9 s step between the RTC and embassy time on boot 27, and whether
  unplugging CN9 with the board running reproduces it. Until CN9's logic lines
  are protected on board A, plug and unplug board B with the 12 V off. Tracked
  in [origin89hq/hardware#8](https://github.com/origin89hq/hardware/issues/8).
- RTS/CTS to the ESP32, and the radios transmitting and joining a network.
